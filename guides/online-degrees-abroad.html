Study Abroad

How universities abroad evaluate online degrees

When you apply abroad, universities don't usually ask "was this online?" They ask what your qualification is equivalent to in their own system. Knowing how that comparison works helps you prepare the right documents and pick programmes you can actually get into.

Last reviewed September 26, 2026

What evaluators look at

Credential evaluators compare your qualification to their national system using a set of factors. UK ENIC, the UK's national agency for this, says it considers:

  • Entry requirements for the course you took,
  • the syllabus you studied,
  • the length of study,
  • and other details, before placing it at the closest equivalent level.

Two things follow from that. First, the institution that awarded your degree and its recognition at home matter more than the delivery format. Second, a shorter degree may be placed differently from a longer one, even at the same title.

Documents that strengthen your application

  1. Official transcripts listing every course, credit, and grade.
  2. Your degree certificate from the awarding institution.
  3. Proof of accreditation: the name of the institution's accreditor, so admissions can verify it quickly.
  4. A course description document if the university asks for syllabus details. Save your course outlines while you still have access to them.
  5. A comparability statement where useful. For the UK, a UK ENIC Statement of Comparability explains how your qualification compares to UK levels.

Tip: many universities publish entry requirements by country. Search the university's site for "international entry requirements" plus the country your degree is from (for BYU-Pathway degrees, that's the United States).

Questions to ask admissions before you apply

  • Does my degree meet the entry requirement for this master's programme?
  • How do you convert my GPA to your grading scale?
  • Do you need a credential evaluation, and from which service?
  • Does my degree's length (number of credits or years) affect eligibility?

Keep the answers in writing. If a scholarship later asks whether you qualify for your chosen programmes, you'll have proof.
