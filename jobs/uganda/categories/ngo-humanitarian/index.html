Reporting to: Voice Programme Manager About Us The Dr. Denis Mukwege Foundation (MF) is an international rights-based, survivor-centred organisation, dedicated to ending sexual violence as a weapon of war globally. The Mukwege Foundation supports survivors’ demands for a world where sexual violence as a weapon of war is no longer tolerated, and bears consequences for individual perpetrators and states. We work for a future where survivors receive the holistic care and compensation they need to rebuild their lives. We create opportunities for survivors to speak out and be heard, and where they can organise to create change, influence policies, and demand justice and accountability. The Mukwege Foundation’s ‘Voice’ programme focuses on strengthening survivors’ agency to actively influence and participate in policy and practice concerning them at national and international levels. At the global level, SEMA – the Global Network of Victims and Survivors to End Wartime Sexual Violence – offers a safe global platform for survivors to come together for solidarity, to share experiences and knowledge, to advocate powerfully for the abolition of the use of sexual violence as a tactic of war, and to lobby for the recognition of the rights of survivors to quality holistic care and to restorative justice. At the national level, the Mukwege Foundation supports the initiation, development, and strengthening of in-country survivor networks, creating safe spaces for peer-to-peer support and building survivors’ capacities to enable them to actively engage in advocating for their rights. The in-country networks vary according to their context and capacities, but in all cases these networks organize to raise awareness about the reality, causes and consequences of wartime rape in the respective countries, to combat stigma, impunity and harmful myths around CRSV, to increase access to survivor-centred holistic care and to commemorate and recognize the many victims of these atrocities by creating a collective memory of survivors’ experiences. The Mukwege Foundation is currently supporting national survivor networks in Bangladesh, Central African Republic, Ethiopia, Iraq, Mali, Myanmar, Nigeria, Nepal, Syria, South Sudan, Uganda, Ukraine, and is in the process of expanding its programme to Chad and Sudan in 2026. Background The conflict in Sudan has been characterised by widespread and systematic conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV), resulting in significant displacement of women, girls, men and children into neighbouring countries. Sudanese survivors of CRSV both within Sudan and in displaced countries face ongoing protection risks including insecurity, stigma, discrimination, retaliation, exploitation, restricted access to services, and limited opportunities to participate safely in decisions affecting their lives. The Dr. Denis Mukwege Foundation has been implementing the ‘Our Voice, Our Justice’ (OVOJ) project from June 2026 up to December 2028. The project is supporting Sudanese refugee CRSV survivors to organise in survivor-led networks, access protection and psychosocial support, and safely engage in advocacy for accountability and justice. The overarching goal of this project is that conflict-related sexual violence survivors from Sudan are protected and supported to safely claim their rights and advocate for justice and accountability through the strengthening of survivor networks. The project is implemented in eastern Chad (in camps in and around Adré), South Sudan (in Juba and in camps around Juba), and Uganda (in the Kiryandongo refugee settlement). Additionally, the Mukwege Foundation is also looking at strategically responding to needs of survivors of conflict-related sexual violence within Sudan through other sources of funding. Hence, Sudan is included in these terms of reference and as part of the overall regional response of the Mukwege Foundation. COUNTRY PROFILE Since 2018, Ethiopia has experienced escalating insecurity and unrest, including large-scale displacement, loss of life, and widespread destruction of property, following significant domestic political changes. These crises have compounded the effects of recurring climate-related shocks affecting large parts of the country. In November 2020, conflict erupted in the northern Tigray region, triggering significant displacement across Tigray, Amhara, and Afar regions. Since August 2023, renewed conflict in Amhara has further intensified humanitarian needs, leading to the declaration of a state of emergency and restrictions on movement across the region. Together, these crises have severely disrupted livelihoods, constrained access to essential goods and staple commodities, and increased the vulnerability of affected populations, placing additional pressure on humanitarian response capacities across the country. The appointment level will depend on the selected candidate’s experience and technical skills, both profiles will carry responsibility for leading the data team. During the assignment, the Senior Data Analyst / Data Specialist will be hosted by Acted, IMPACT's global partner, and will be under the responsibility of its delegates for all administrative, security, logistics and financial matters. Background Under its Climate Change Adaptation and Mitigation strategic priority area, CRS Uganda supports nature-based solutions, climate resilience and sustainable livelihoods. Forest Flavors (FF) is a 3-year initiative (FY26 - FY29) that applies a market systems development (MSD) and private-sector-led approach to strengthen inclusive, commercially viable and nature-positive agroforestry value chains, with an initial focus on coffee, cocoa, vanilla and spices. The project seeks to improve smallholder and dioceses market participation and returns, support enterprises to test and scale inclusive business models, and strengthen climate-resilient agroforestry landscapes. Forest Flavors will work mainly across 3 regions namely, Central region covering Luwero, Masaka and Lugazi, Western region covering Kasese and Northern region covering Gulu directly engaging with private sector actors, smallholder farmers and their organizations, service providers, financial institutions and relevant public and civil-society stakeholders to address market-system constraints and crowd in sustainable investment. Church and diocesan assets where feasible will contribute to viable business and landscape models where land tenure, governance, user rights, market demand and the business case are validated. The project will use a diagnose, prioritize, co-create, invest, learn and scale approach, with decisions guided by commercial viability, inclusion, environmental contribution and the potential for replication.
